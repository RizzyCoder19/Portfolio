# Product Vision — Portfolio as Product

Date: 2026-07-28
Owner: RizzyCoder19

## Purpose
This portfolio's primary product is *you* — not a resume, not a list of skills, but an authored, cinematic experience that communicates identity, thinking, and future direction. The site itself is the flagship case study: it must be a meaningful artefact that convinces recruiters and hiring leads that the creator is hireable by virtue of the craft shown here.

## Core Proposition
- The portfolio is the product. The narrative, motion, layout, and interactions are the case study.
- Goal: When a recruiter experiences the site, they think: “Even without many projects, this person is worth hiring.”
- Audience: Design leads, engineering leads, product hiring managers, recruiters at boutique studios and high-growth startups.

## Key Content Pillars
1. Me — a personal, narrative-led introduction focused on identity and intent (not a bullet resume). 
2. Flagship case study — this portfolio itself, documented: design decisions, motion language, architecture, experiments as research. 
3. Design thinking — process and choices, annotated with rationale. 
4. Engineering philosophy — tradeoffs, performance, accessibility, and how those inform UI choices. 
5. Future direction — areas you want to grow into and what you’re seeking.

Supporting artifacts (secondary): Resume, GitHub, LinkedIn, Email — clearly accessible but subordinate to the experience.

## Tone & Visual Language
- Preserve and evolve the *Royal Dark Romance* aesthetic from the intro across the entire site.
- Attributes: editorial, cinematic, mysterious, elegant, immersive.
- Interactions: slow, deliberate pacing; reveal and cadence; subtle transform + opacity animation; reduced-motion fallback.
- Color & typography: high-contrast type, generous negative space, restrained accent color (signal beam) for CTAs and focus.

## Interaction & UX Principles
- Experience-first: every page supports a readable narrative flow. 
- Cinematic framing: large photography/portrait options, generous margins, and typographic hierarchy.
- Focused choices: minimal navigation options, clear primary CTA (hire/contact). 
- Accessibility-first: keyboard paths, visible focus, semantic markup, color contrast.

## Content Map (priority order)
1. Intro / Hero — identity-first, short narrative line, cinematic portrait, primary CTA to "Work / Case Study". 
2. Case Study (this site) — in-depth, with subsections: Challenge, Design System, Motion Language, Architecture, Accessibility, Outcome. 
3. About — deeper personal narrative, thinking, and orientation. 
4. Work — curated other projects (if present), each with short narrative and link to details. 
5. Experiments — reposition as research playground / process snapshots; curated and intentionally selected. 
6. Contact — prominent, trust-building, explicit "hire me" CTA plus supporting links.

## Success Metrics (qualitative + quantitative)
- Recruiter reaction: positive subjective feedback (aim: "wow" responses). 
- Contact conversions: clicks / messages to email or contact form. 
- Time on page and scroll depth for the case study. 
- Resume downloads / GitHub referrals.
